# Raider Observation Digest

Strips an iObservation report down to what was actually scored, plus everything your observer wrote, on one page you can print — for Regis Jesuit High School teachers.

**[Set up Raider Observation Digest →](https://rjedtech.github.io/Raider-Observation-Digest/)**

## The problem

An iObservation report lists **41 teaching elements**, each with a row of evidence checkboxes, a six-point scale and a training video. Scroll one and you pass several hundred empty boxes.

In a typical formal observation, **about five of those 41 were actually scored.** The other thirty-six were left blank, because one adult watching one class period cannot see 41 things. Blank is not a zero — it means that element wasn't what the visit was looking at.

So the report is five elements and a paragraph of writing, wrapped in a form built to cover every possibility. Most teachers close the tab.

## What it does

One click on a page you already have open gives you:

- **Only the elements that were scored** — the other 36 and their empty checkboxes removed
- **The level ladder** for each, with the rung you were given filled in
- **The evidence your observer actually ticked**, split into what they saw you do and what they saw students do
- **Any per-element comments**, and everything written in Overall Comments, in full
- **Your own acknowledgement reply**, if you left one
- **Print / Save as PDF** that drops the interface and prints clean — one element per block, nothing split across pages
- **Light and dark** — follows your computer’s setting; the toggle on the setup page overrides it and remembers

Press the bookmark again, or `Esc`, or **Close**, and you're back on the full report. Nothing about it is changed.

## Why a bookmark and not a web page

The report lives behind your login on the vendor's site. A separate web page can't read it, and Canvas strips `javascript:` links out of pages, so the button can't live in the PD course either. A bookmark whose address is code is the only way to run something against a page you're already looking at without installing an extension.

## Privacy

- No accounts, no login, no ads, no cookies, no tracking
- No data leaves the browser — it reads the page already rendered on your screen
- Nothing is stored, and nothing is transmitted anywhere, ever
- It cannot edit, submit or change your observation — it only reformats the view
- Nothing is logged, so there is no way for anyone to tell you used it

## How to use

1. Visit the link above.
2. Press `Ctrl` `Shift` `B` to show your bookmarks bar.
3. **Drag** the button into the bookmarks bar — don't click it.
4. In iObservation, open **Observations → Completed Observations** and **View** your report.
5. Click **Observation Digest** on your bookmarks bar.

The setup is a one-time, one-minute job. After that it's one click per report. Bring a laptop to an Educational Technology drop-in if you'd rather someone did it with you.

## What to do with the report

Reading it is the easy half. The Professional Development course covers the harder half — answering your observer's questions in the acknowledgement box, taking one element up a rung instead of five, treating the 36 blanks as a menu rather than a verdict, and reading an observation next to what your students said.

[Reading Your Observation Report → (course 5414)](https://rjhs.instructure.com/courses/5414/pages/reading-your-observation-report)

## Notes

- **Tested on:** formal observations on the Marzano-style *Standard 3: Models Ignatian Pedagogy* form.
- **Not yet tested on:** walkthrough and informal observations, or reports that carry per-element comments. They should work; they haven't been confirmed.
- The tool reads the vendor's own page structure. A vendor redesign can break it — when that happens it shows *"Nothing to summarize"* with instructions rather than a blank screen. Tell Educational Technology and it gets fixed.
- It deliberately does **not** paraphrase what the scale levels mean. That wording belongs to the observation framework and sits behind the **View Scale** button on the report.
